# Premier League Analysis — Insights

Two full seasons of Premier League matches (2023/24 and 2024/25), 760 matches
and 2,361 goals in total. Every number below comes out of `analysis.py` and
`queries.sql` run on the raw data from football-data.co.uk.

## Goals dropped off in 2024/25

- 2023/24 averaged **3.28 goals per match** (1,246 goals). 2024/25 averaged
  **2.93** (1,115 goals) — about one goal fewer in every three matches.
- The fall came mostly from home teams: home goals per match dropped from
  1.80 to 1.51, while away goals barely moved (1.48 to 1.42).
- The highest-scoring month in the whole dataset was **February 2024 at 3.76
  goals per match**. The top five scoring months are all from the second
  half of the 2023/24 season.

## Home advantage is shrinking

- 2023/24: home teams won **46.1%** of matches, away teams 32.4%.
- 2024/25: home wins fell to **40.8%**, away wins rose to 34.7%, and draws
  went up from 21.6% to 24.5%.
- Across both seasons combined, playing at home still helps — 43.4% home
  wins against 33.6% away wins — but the gap closed a lot in one year.

## Attack and defence

- Best attack 2023/24: **Manchester City, 96 goals**. Best attack 2024/25:
  **Liverpool, 86 goals**.
- Across the two seasons combined, Liverpool scored the most goals of any
  team: **172**, ahead of Man City (168) and Arsenal (160).
- Best defence both seasons: **Arsenal** — only 29 goals conceded in
  2023/24 and 34 in 2024/25.
- Arsenal also kept the most clean sheets across the two seasons (31),
  followed by Man City (26) and Everton (25).

## Liverpool

- 2023/24: **3rd place, 82 points** (W24 D10 L4). 2024/25: **champions,
  84 points** (W25 D9 L4).
- Strange but true: Liverpool scored **86 goals and conceded 41 in both
  seasons** — identical attack and defence numbers, but two more points
  and a title the second time.
- Anfield was a fortress: Liverpool lost only **one home match in each
  season**. Combined home record over the two seasons: W29 D7 L2, against
  W20 D12 L6 away.
- What changed in the title season was the defence keeping clean sheets:
  10 in 2023/24, a league-best **14 in 2024/25**.
- 15 of Liverpool's 76 matches (about 1 in 5) had 5 or more total goals —
  win or lose, they were rarely boring to watch.

## What I take from this

The story of these two seasons in one line: the league got tighter and
lower-scoring, home advantage faded, and the title was won by the team
whose attack stayed at exactly the same level while its defence gave away
fewer goals in the matches that mattered.
